The Engine

It already
runs.

Hawaii worked because of the weeks before it. Same engine, eight more weeks, higher bar.

Four days of straight dancing in Waikiki went fine, and none of that came from the four days. It came from a schedule that was already running every week. The curveballs, the unfamiliar follows, the styles I had not seen before, all of it was survivable because the reps were already banked.

7
Out every night. Four in a classroom, three on a floor. Monday through Thursday is class. Friday through Sunday is the floor. That is the whole week, with the Saturday 1 PM block sitting on top in the afternoon rather than taking a night. Every social opens with a club lesson I take too, so the teaching hours land around eight a week even though only five classes show on a schedule. The system works. I just have to hold it for eight more weeks.
Mon to Thu

Weeknights. The classes.

MON
7:15 Bachata Footwork
8:00 Social Dance Confidence
Phoenix Bachata Dance Academy, 3302 N 3rd St. Both, open level, locked. The 8:00 block is a Q&A, and it is quietly the most valuable hour of the week because I walk in with a real problem off Saturday night. The follow read cuddle when you led shadow. That kind of thing gets answered.
TUE
8:00 Salsa, Cavallo
or 8:00 + 9:30 Bachata, PBD
Contested. Both studios put their good class at 8. Sotol runs a social after if there is anything left in the legs.
WED
7:00 Team Training
8:00 Rueda
8:45 Social
Modern Bachata Fusion Season 4, twelve weeks from Sep 2, then rueda, then the Latin Fusion Social. Longest night of the week, and the one to be honest about. Rueda is mostly reps: beginner-intermediate, same 70 to 75 calls, one new one a week. It sharpens timing under call pressure and adds almost no vocabulary. The team may have the same ceiling for a different reason, since a co-ed team with a handful of strong leads gets choreographed to the median lead. Worth checking before I assume it. If it holds, the real stretch nights are Thursday and Cavallo, and Wednesday is volume.

Footwork and weight
Pendulum weight transfer Implemented
TaughtSink down into the right hip. Then drive the weight across, pushing that hip as far as it will go, until I feel it in the obliques. The rest of the body follows after. It arrives last, it does not lead.
Why it mattersThis is the engine under two other items on the list. "Push, do not lift" and "ground up, not chest" are the same mechanism seen from different angles. And underneath the anatomy it is a timing instruction: hip first, body second.
The basic Implemented
WasStarting the basic by stepping with the left leg and lifting it.
NowPush off the right leg. The push is what lets the hips carry the movement instead of the legs just relocating me. Hips go right on 1, left on 5.
Emphasis on 1 and 5 Implemented
WasHitting 1 and 5 the same weight as everything else, so a follow gets no landmark.
NowCarlitos flagged it: emphasize the 1 and the 5 with a follow. Not louder footwork, more visible weight arriving. Those are the two counts she reorients off, and they are the same two counts the hips already turn on, right on 1 and left on 5.
The tap Implemented
WasPushing far too hard into the tap. Enough force that the other leg bounced with it.
NowGoing left on 1-4, only the right hip and glute elevate, and all the weight sits in the right leg for 1-4. Mirrored for the back half.
Connection and clarity
Preps Implemented
WasPrepping in the wrong direction, or prepping at the last second. Both kill clarity.
NowPrep early, and prep the direction I actually mean. The purest timing fix on the list.
The forward-and-back prep, count by countSending her backwards: prep on 2 and 3, have her wrist by 3 and 4, turn by 4. Nothing in that instruction but counts, which is this whole section in one line.
Right turn, both turning Implemented
WasNot enough contact with the follow through the turn.
NowContact through the arms and body right before the free spin. Off count 4, or off count 8 to turn the other direction.
Hand signals in footwork Implemented
WasWrong signal. On 1, 2, jump on 3, then 4 tapped with the foot in front of the left and vice versa, she had nothing clear to read.
NowThumb into the middle of her palm. That is the information. Same principle for an open-close, where the sliding foot is matched by the hand. Same for hips, both hands, up and down.
Saturday Sep 12, and I could hear itA well-known AZ bachata teacher ran this at a social and called the count wrong. Open-close slide on the left foot across 1&2, then he labeled the next part 3&4 when it was 3, 4. Two weight changes, not three, so the ampersand promised a syncopation that was never there. He finished it with a body wave in open position, where there is no frame to send it through. Worth logging because six months ago I would have copied it. Now the wrong count is the first thing I notice.
The two-finger grip Getting there
WasBreaking the middle and ring finger spiderman grip while initiating a simple follow left turn on count 5, leaving her nothing to hold.
NowHold it through the turn. She cannot follow a connection that disappears at the moment she needs it. Not always perfect yet. The last thing on the list.
Frame and travel
Half-open frame Implemented
WasFrame breaking in half-open, then collapsing.
NowHolds. Cece called it out in the first private, and I have been drilling it since.
Shoulders Implemented
WasShoulders drifting, so the basic wandered off its line.
NowShoulders straight and centered, which is what keeps the basic travelling in a line with a follow on it.
Breaking the 1-8 Implemented
WasLosing the direction after anything that interrupts a full 1-8.
NowAfter a half basic on 5-8, or an infinity in half-open for four counts, keep going in the 5-8 direction, to the right. For the direction, the timing is what matters. If I get off center anyway, I step-tap, step-tap, or in half-open I move her frame back and forth, up and down, for a full 4 count to get back on track.
Sensual
Sensual timing Implemented
WasRushing sensual moves into 4 counts.
NowTaking the full 8 counts, which fixed the rushing. It also created a new problem at first: the follow read 4, committed to an exit, and pulled back on me around 4. So I was mid-move with her weight already leaving.
What that pull-back is telling meShe is not guessing. She is answering the last thing I sent her, and the last thing I sent her was at 4. A prep says something is starting. It does not say how long. If my connection goes neutral after count 4 she has no reason to think the move is still running, so she recovers, which is the correct thing for a good follow to do. Two halves to this: prep the length, not just the direction, and keep sending through 5 to 8 instead of going quiet once the move is underway.
Where it comes from Implemented
WasDriving sensual movement from the chest.
NowGround up. Floor, legs, hips, and the upper body arrives last.
Accents Implemented
WasCatching musical accents with the upper body only.
NowBody syncopations come from the hips. The guitar note in Te Extraño is the reference.
Salsa privates with Leela at Cavallo
Same note, different room
LeelaMore contact with the follow, not less. Word for word the thing Cece and Carlitos said in bachata, arrived at independently, in a different style, with a different teacher.
The combo, in order
  1. Half basic
  2. Lead right to follow's left
  3. Shoulder check
  4. Follow right turn
  5. Hair comb
  6. Lead left to follow's right hand
  7. Crossbody spin
  8. Contact the shoulderblade on count 6
  9. Crossbody
Why it is one note and not two
ReadNeither teacher asked for more contact in the abstract. Both handed over a count. Bachata: arms and body right before the free spin, off 4 or off 8. Salsa: shoulderblade on 6.
And where they put itBoth land the contact immediately before or during a turn, which is the one moment she cannot see me and the hand is her only channel. The body contact is what tells her where here is when she comes out of it. Miss the count and she lands on her own and guesses. Which makes it a timing item too, exactly like Cece said. "More contact" is the symptom. The instruction is the count.
The drill worth keeping

They pressed their rear shoulder into my sternum and had me follow off their movement, then swapped. Enlightening from both sides, and the most portable thing that came out of those four hours. Sternum contact is the channel a follow actually reads in close and half-open, so standing on her side of it tells me how much information I am actually sending. Run it before socials. Costs one song, and it tells me fast whether my prep is early enough.

Thirteen of fourteen are in my dancing now. The grip is the one left. It holds most turns, not every one. When it breaks at count 5, she has nothing to hold right when she needs it. Same note Cece and Leela each gave me separately: more contact, on a count.
