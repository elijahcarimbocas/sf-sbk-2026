Eighth year running. The biggest SBK weekend in the country.

Salsa, bachata, kizomba and Brazilian zouk, all under one roof at the Hyatt Regency SFO. Workshops from 10 AM, afternoon socials, shows every night, and the floor stays open until roughly 7 in the morning. Artists and DJs fly in from Europe, Asia and Latin America. Three competitions run alongside it.

The man who rated the room is teaching in it. Carlitos and CeCe are on the SBK bill with Modern Bachata Fusion and Rides & Counterbalances. The Engine

It already
runs.

Hawaii worked because of the weeks before it. Same engine, eight more weeks, higher bar.

Four days of straight dancing in Waikiki went fine, and none of that came from the four days. It came from a schedule that was already running every week. The curveballs, the unfamiliar follows, the styles he had not seen before, all of it was survivable because the reps were already banked.

7
Out every night. Four in a classroom, three on a floor. Monday through Thursday is class. Friday through Sunday is the floor. That is the whole week, with the Saturday 1 PM block sitting on top in the afternoon rather than taking a night. Every social opens with a club lesson he takes too, so the teaching hours land around eight a week even though only five classes appear on a schedule. The system does not need reinventing for this trip. It needs holding.

The floor runs 1.25 follows to every lead, and it is worse at a congress. Beginner leads quit at a much higher rate around month two, the programming leans follow-side (ladies styling, heels, more styling), and follows travel for dance more because at home they sit out. Two things fall out of that. The strong follows in the room have gaps too, so ask above your level constantly, the worst case is a polite no. And nobody is ever going to make you rest. Four nights to 7 AM with a standing invitation to dance every song is how you arrive Sunday with nothing left. Pacing has to be a decision.

Every social opens with a club lesson, and he takes all of them. Warm up, get the body moving before the floor fills, and steal a technique or two off an instructor he does not train with weekly. That is three extra hours of teaching a week that never shows up on a studio schedule, and it is a big part of why the Hawaii floor felt survivable. Different teacher every night means different vocabulary, which is exactly the problem a 6-8 room hands you.
